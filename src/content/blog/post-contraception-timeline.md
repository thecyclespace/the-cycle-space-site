Stopping hormonal contraception or removing an IUD is a real shift for the body. The cycle takes time to find its rhythm again, and that timeline is **different for everyone**.

This guide is built to help you make sense of what's happening — not to predict it. Pick what you stopped or removed, pick how long ago, and you'll see what tends to be common at that stage and what may be worth noticing.

<!-- calculator -->

## A few principles behind this guide

- **The body adapts at its own pace.** Some people resume regular cycles within weeks, others take several months. Both are normal.
- **Variability is information, not failure.** Irregular cycles in the first few months after stopping are common — they don't necessarily mean something is wrong.
- **Underlying patterns become visible.** Conditions or symptoms that the contraception was masking (PMS, painful periods, acne, mood swings) may resurface. They're not caused by stopping — they were just hidden.
- **You know your body best.** This guide gives you general patterns. Your gut feeling about what's normal *for you* matters.

## What this guide is — and isn't

- It is **educational** — synthesised from sources like the NHS, ACOG, NICHD and the Cleveland Clinic.
- It is **not a diagnosis** and not a contraceptive method.
- It is **not a substitute** for a conversation with a healthcare professional, especially if something feels wrong.
