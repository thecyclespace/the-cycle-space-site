A "regular" cycle is often described as 28 days — but real cycles vary widely. Anywhere from **21 to 35 days** is generally considered within a normal range, and **a few days of variation** between cycles is completely normal.

This tool looks at your **last 3 to 6 period start dates** and tells you what your average cycle has been, how much it's been varying, and whether that variability falls into a typical range.

<!-- calculator -->

## How does this work?

The tool sorts your dates, calculates the gap in days between each consecutive pair, then computes:

- **Average cycle length** (the mean of those gaps)
- **Shortest** and **longest** cycle in your data
- **Variability** (longest − shortest)

It then places your pattern into a soft category:

- **Likely regular** — variability of 7 days or less.
- **Slightly variable** — variability of 8 to 14 days.
- **More variable** — variability of more than 14 days.

These thresholds reflect general patterns described by sources like the NHS, ACOG and the Office on Women's Health. They are **educational guides**, not medical thresholds.

## What can cause variability

Cycle length can shift for many reasons that have nothing to do with illness:

- Stress and sleep
- Travel and time-zone changes
- Recent weight loss or gain
- Stopping hormonal contraception
- Breastfeeding
- Hormonal transitions (puberty, perimenopause)

It can also reflect underlying conditions like PCOS, thyroid imbalances or endometriosis — but those are diagnosed by a professional, not by an online tool.

## When it's worth talking to someone

If your cycles are often very short (under 21 days), very long (over 35 days), absent, very heavy, or very painful — or if a sudden change worries you — it's reasonable to bring that up with a healthcare professional.
